# ROI — World Lore & Visual Reference

A worldbuilding document focused on **visual atmosphere, named entities,
heraldic motifs, and palette** — meant to feed AI image generators
(Midjourney, DALL-E, SD, Flux) for emblems, location art, character
portraits, faction badges, item icons, etc.

For full game mechanics see `GDD.md`. For per-enemy stats see
`bestiary.md`. For per-recipe ingredients see `recipes.md`. This file is
the **flavour layer** — what the kingdom looks, smells and feels like.

---

## 1. The Kingdom of Artania

**Artania** (Ukr: *Артанія*) is a Slavic-flavoured medieval kingdom — a
realm of dense temperate pine and oak forests, slow rivers, peat bogs,
and stone-built capital towns. Climate is mild summer / cold-but-snow-light
winter; the foliage tilts toward dark greens with warm amber undertones,
especially in the long late-summer-into-autumn light the world seems to
live in.

Architecture across the realm is honest carpentry and fieldstone — log
cabins, plank-and-thatch barns, mortared rough-cut walls — softening
into proper dressed-stone keeps only in the capital and the great
houses. Bright pigments are rare; the dominant palette is **forest
green, weathered timber brown, fieldstone grey, late-amber gold, and
the deep dark of the woods beyond the firelight**.

The kingdom is old enough to feel lived-in but not so old it has
ossified. Roads are well-trodden but unpaved between settlements;
bridges are stone-pylon + wooden-deck; lanterns are oil-and-tin.

## 2. The Blight — *Beastfever* (Звірячий Сказ)

The kingdom is sick. A creeping rabies-like plague — *Beastfever*,
*Звірячий Сказ* — has spread through the wildlife of the kingdom's
forests. Infected creatures become gaunt, foam-mouthed, hyper-aggressive,
and lose all natural caution around humans. The forests, once a source
of game and timber, have become a low-grade siege the kingdom is
losing tree-line by tree-line.

**Visual cues of Beastfever (for any infected creature or symbol of the
blight):**
- bone-pale matted fur with greasy reddish-black streaks
- swollen joints, prominent ribs, hunched gait
- eyes glassy with too-bright pinprick pupils, often a faint sickly
  greenish-yellow sclera
- white foam around the muzzle, sometimes flecked with darker bile
- shed patches of fur exposing greyish raw skin
- a faint vapour or steam rising from the body even in mild weather —
  the fever runs hot
- claws and teeth disproportionately long, as if the body keeps
  growing what it needs to bite

The Blight does not infect humans (the lore reason the player can
fight rabid wolves bare-handed without fear). Why is not explained
in-game — old wisdom says the Crown wears a charm, but no one is
certain. It DOES rot the meat of infected creatures (rabid family
drops only hide, never meat).

**The visual language of the Blight is fungal, feverish, and damp** —
not skeletal or demonic. Think rotting log moss, not skulls; think
sweat-soaked, not zombified.

## 3. The Crown & the Office of Governor

The kingdom is ruled by **the King** (always referred to by title, never
named) from a tall pale-stone royal palace in the capital. The Crown
watches the slow loss of the forests with a mix of pragmatism and dread,
and answers by appointing **Governors** (Ukr: *Намісники*) to outlying
plots — young soldiers, archers and mages who swear the **King's Oath**
in person, receive a starter weapon, a wooden hut on a clearing, and
instructions to rebuild the land and cleanse it of the blight.

The player is one of these Governors. NPCs always address them as
"Governor" / "Наміснику" — the rank carries real weight even when the
estate is still a one-room hut.

**Visual identity of the Crown:**
- Heraldic colour: deep forest green and aged-gold — same warm amber
  as the late-summer light, never bright primary yellow
- Emblem: a stylised pine crown over a wolfshead (wolf as both old
  enemy and old totem of the kingdom) — pine for the forests the
  Crown is sworn to protect, wolf for the price of failing
- The royal seal appears on the **King's Charter** (red wax, pine-and-wolf
  impression) shown to every new Governor at registration

**Visual identity of a Governor:**
- A simple woven sash in Crown green crossing the chest, regardless of
  class or rank
- A small bronze pin of the pine crown worn at the collar
- Otherwise wears whatever their class and means allow — gear, not livery

## 4. The Three Paths (Player Classes)

Every Governor swears the same oath but walks one of three paths,
chosen at the King's instruction:

### Knight (Warrior — *Лицар*)
The unbreakable shield of the kingdom. Heavy chain or scale over
gambeson, tower or kite shield, longsword or arming sword. Visual
palette: muted steel, oiled leather, deep red wool surcoat. Stance: feet
planted, blade ready, expression patient. Symbol: **a tower shield
crossed by a longsword, on a field of Crown green**.

### Archer (*Лучник*)
The keen eye of Artania. Layered hunter's leathers (forester-green
jerkin, hide bracers, soft-soled boots), longbow or recurve, full quiver
across the back, a hood that shadows the face. Visual palette: forest
greens, weathered browns, oxblood accents on the leather. Stance: half-turned,
ready to step away or loose, eyes already on the next target. Symbol:
**a yew longbow drawn taut, with a single arrow nocked, against a field
of Crown green**.

### Mage (*Маг*)
A wanderer of the old ways, trained in arts the Crown tolerates because
the Blight is worse. Long woollen robes belted at the waist, hood up
in the cold, a carved wooden staff with a crystal or worked-iron head,
amulets at the throat. Visual palette: dusk-purple, twilight-blue, ash
grey, with cold blue-white highlights where magic is implied. Stance:
upright, hands free, staff planted, watching the air for things others
don't see. Symbol: **a wooden staff with a crystal head, framed by a
crescent moon and three stars, on a field of Crown green**.

All three class symbols share the same Crown-green field — they are
all sworn to the same throne.

## 5. The Estate — Seven Tiers of a Governor's Rise

A Governor begins with a wilderness clearing, a wooden hut, and a
royal charter. Through trade, craft, and slow patient war against the
Blight they raise the estate through seven tiers. Each tier is a real,
physical evolution of the same plot of land — the original hut never
quite disappears, it just becomes part of something larger.

| Tier | Name | What it looks like |
|------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | Wooden Hut | A single rough log hut on a half-cleared patch. Stumps still everywhere. Axe leaning against the wall. The forest crowds right up to the door. |
| 2 | Settler's Cabin | Two-room log cabin, wattle fence, vegetable patch, well, woodpile. The clearing is properly cleared now. |
| 3 | Forester's Lodge | Two-storey timber house on a fieldstone foundation, small workshop shed with a forge stub, low fieldstone perimeter wall, modest garden. First sign of permanence. |
| 4 | Manor | Whitewashed plaster-and-timber manor with clay-tile roof, ringed by a proper stone wall and small gatehouse. Outbuildings: tannery, forge, granary. A young orchard. |
| 5 | Knight's Hold | The manor grows a stone watchtower at one corner with arrow slits and conical slate roof. Crenellated perimeter wall, banner above the gate, small chapel with wooden bell. |
| 6 | Baron's Seat | A fortified residence approaching small-castle scale. Two flanking towers, barbican gatehouse with portcullis, stone-flagged courtyard, deeper orchards, stone bell tower. |
| 7 | Lord's Holdings | A grand fortified estate — square keep with machicolations, great hall with stained-glass, curtain wall with four towers, twin-towered gatehouse with drawbridge over a dry moat, formal walled garden, cloistered walks. Endgame seat of a regional lord. |

The progression is **always the same plot of land**, in the same
clearing, surrounded by the same dark forest — only the buildings,
walls, and grounds evolve. For art purposes this means: keep the
forest line, the camera angle, the time of day, and the season
identical across the tier set.

## 6. The Capital — Artania City

The kingdom's only proper city. Stone-walled, river-fed, gate-guarded.
Travel from any estate is two days on horseback (in-game compressed to
a 2-minute trip). The capital is where the Crown sits, where coin
changes hands, and where every Governor visits to trade, drink, gamble,
and listen to their fate read in the cards.

Inside the walls, the player can visit six places:

### 🛒 The Bazaar / Ринок *(planned)*
A bustling stall-market lining the main square. Player-to-player trade,
when it ships. Visual notes: striped canvas awnings in faded green and
rust, stacked baskets, hanging dried herbs, the smell of bread and
woodsmoke implied by the haze.

### ⚔️ The Arena / Арена *(planned)*
A round sand-floored fighting pit ringed by wooden tiers, sealed gate
with the Crown's wax. Posted rules of single combat. Old dark stains on
the sand. Sober, not gladiatorial — duels here are formal, not
spectacle.

### 💼 The Trader / Крамар (live)
A long-running merchant's shop behind a worn counter, wares stacked
floor to rafters: barrels of pebbles and lumber, sacks of berries and
nuts, slabs of cured meat hanging from hooks, baskets of duck eggs,
ingots and lumps of iron in wooden trays. The Trader himself is wiry,
silver-haired, sharp-eyed, fair scales. He buys what the Governor
doesn't need and sells what they can't easily find. Honest scales,
honest deals, no friend to thieves.

### 🔮 The Fortune Teller / Ворожка (live, Phase 6.4)
A small dim room behind a thick wool curtain, smelling of beeswax,
dried lavender, and the cedar of her card-chest. An old woman in
indigo wool with silver thread at the cuffs, eyes paler than they
should be. A deck of 22 Major Arcana tarot cards spread on a worn
oak table by candlelight. For a few silvers she will let you draw
one card; it will shape the next six hours of your fate. She is older
than she should be. The cards know your name before you sit down.

### 🛠 The Master / Майстер *(planned)*
A workshop louder than a forge — wood, iron, leather and glass all
under one roof. The Master is grey-bearded, soot-streaked, blunt. When
weapon repair, reforging, and enchanting ship, they live here.

### 🍺 The Tavern — *"Royal Seal"* / Шинок «Королівська печатка» (live)
The kingdom's most beloved inn, named for the wax seal of the Crown
that hangs framed above the hearth (a gift from the King for some
forgotten favour). A long, low room, smoke-blackened beams, a great
stone fireplace, long oak tables polished by years of elbows. The
Innkeeper is broad-shouldered, ruddy-faced, perpetually amused. He
sells seven cooked dishes (baked potato → governor's feast) and runs
two friendly gambles: **dice** and **darts**. A tankard of dark ale is
always within reach. NPCs and Governors mix freely — this is the one
place in the capital where rank is left at the door.

**Architecture of the capital generally:**
Pale dressed-stone walls, slate or clay-tile roofs, narrow streets
flagged with worn paving stones, wooden shop signs hanging from
wrought-iron brackets, glazed leadlight windows on the wealthier
houses, oil lanterns at every corner. The royal palace dominates the
skyline — a tall pale tower with the green-and-gold banner of the
Crown flying from its top.

## 7. The Wilderness — Beyond the Estate

Past the perimeter of any estate, the kingdom thins into deep forest
and then into the wilderness proper. Wilderness is measured in
kilometres of *depth* from the homestead (1 km = a long walk to the
treeline; 49 km = the horizon of the world, where a level-40 beast is the
deepest thing that can live). Foraging,
encounters, and ambient resources scale by depth.

**Three wilderness zones for art reference.** The bands are the ones
`zones.json` and the bestiary both use — one ladder, three names, aligned in
Phase 9. Depths were 1–10 / 10–20 / 20–35 before the level cap moved to 40.

### Thicket — Гущавина (1–10 km)
Mixed pine-and-oak forest, dappled sunlight reaching the floor, soft
moss underfoot, bracken and bilberry bushes, fallen logs already half
returned to soil. Birds heard but not seen. Streams clean enough to
drink. The blight is rumour here. Atmosphere: warm, lived-in, dangerous
but recoverable.

### Old Wood — Старий ліс (11–25 km)
Pine-dominant, taller and denser, less light reaching the ground, thick
needle-carpet, the occasional clearing of bone-pale grass where
something died. First sightings of Blight-touched undergrowth — fungal
patches, oddly twisted saplings. Streams running darker. Atmosphere:
quiet, wary, the kind of quiet where you stop talking.

### Deep Wood — Пуща (26–49 km)
Old-growth oak, towering and silent, almost no understorey, a forest
floor of dry leaves and exposed roots like ribs. Light is amber-tinted
and slanted even at noon. The Blight is everywhere — bark peeling in
sheets, sap running black, the air faintly hot and damp. Streams
foul. Atmosphere: oppressive, beautiful, fundamentally not a place
humans should be.

## 8. The Beasts

Two families of wildlife, and one stand-in.

### The Wild Family (uninfected, drop meat + hide)
- **Wild Boar** 🐗 — squat, ferocious, shallow-zone king. Coarse dark
  bristles, curved tusks, small hot eyes.
- **Wild Moose** 🫎 — tall, regal, palm-antlered. Velvet-dark coat,
  long melancholy face. Will charge if cornered.
- **Wild Bison** 🦬 — heavy-shouldered, shaggy, slow but
  immovable. The kingdom's old herd-beast turned wild.
- **Wild Bear** 🐻 — full-grown brown bear, deep-chested, claws like
  carving knives. Deep-zone apex of the uninfected wild.

These animals are dangerous but *clean* — their bodies are meat for
the table, their hides are leather for the tannery, their bones useful.
Killing them is a hunter's pride, not a crusade.

### The Rabid Family (Blight-infected, drop hide only — meat is poisoned)
- **Rabid Lynx** 🐈‍⬛ — once a forest cat, now a fevered streak of
  matted fur and too-bright eyes. Faster than it should be.
- **Rabid Wolf** 🐺 — the symbol of the Blight. Once-grey coat now
  patchy and yellow-streaked, foam on the muzzle, ribs showing,
  eyes unblinking. The first creature the player ever fights at
  registration.
- **Rabid Bear** 🐻‍❄️ — a bear in the late stages of the disease.
  Fur half-shed, raw greyish skin showing through, gait halting but
  the bite still lethal. The kingdom's nightmare made flesh.

These creatures are not enemies in the heroic sense — they are
*patients past saving*. The Governor kills them out of mercy and
necessity, never glory.

### The Training Dummy
A man-sized figure of straw, burlap and lashed pine staves, set up at
the Training Ground plot. Painted with a crude target on the chest in
red ochre. Auto-revives between sparring rounds. The Master's apprentices
build them in batches.

## 9. Economy, Craft & the Coin

The kingdom runs on **silvers** (Ukr: *срібники*) — slim, slightly
worn coins of moderately pure silver, stamped on one face with the
pine-and-wolf of the Crown and on the other with a numeral. They
clink softly, tarnish to dull grey in a purse, and feel cool in the
hand. The Trader counts them with a thumb-flick that comes from
years of practice.

**Gold exists in the kingdom but isn't player currency** — gold is
reserved for crown-level transactions, foreign embassies, and treasury
ledgers. A Governor handles silvers all their life.

### Materials a Governor handles
- 🪨 **River Pebble** — water-worn smooth stones, the basic mason's filler
- 🌲 **Pine Lumber** — squared timber from felled pines, every craft starts here
- 🧱 **Clay** — pale plastic clay dug from damp banks, for plaster and tile
- 🦴 **Hide** — tanned beast skin, ready for the leather workbench
- 🔩 **Iron Lump** — raw iron, fist-sized, dull grey with rust streaks
- 🔳 **Iron Ingot** — refined iron, hand-length, hammered smooth (10 lumps → 1 ingot at the forge)

### Workshops
- **🔥 Forge** — open-hearth smithy. Bellows, anvil, glowing iron, sparks. Smelts ingots, will eventually upgrade weapons. Smell: hot iron and pine smoke.
- **🧵 Tannery** — drying racks, scraping benches, vats of bark-tea
  liquor. Turns hide into the Forester's leather set (hood, jerkin,
  breeches, boots). Smell: oak bark, smoke, dampness.
- **🍳 Kitchen** — wood-fired hearth, copper pots, hanging herbs,
  cutting board worn by years of knives. Turns raw ingredients +
  scroll-learned recipes into vigour-restoring dishes. Smell: woodsmoke,
  onion, rendered fat.
- **📦 Warehouse** — long stone-floored storeroom with shelves and
  numbered barrels. The Governor's bulk stockpile. Smell: dust, sacking,
  dry timber.

### Crafted Gear (Forester's Leather Set)
A matched set of practical wilderness leather in muted forest greens
and oiled browns — hood, jerkin, breeches, boots. The set is the
midgame "you've earned it" armour for any class. Visual cue: a faint
pine-needle stitch motif along the hems.

## 10. The Tarot — The Fortune Teller's Deck

The Fortune Teller's deck is the **22 Major Arcana** of the
traditional tarot — the Fool, the Magician, the High Priestess, the
Empress, the Emperor, the Hierophant, the Lovers, the Chariot,
Strength, the Hermit, the Wheel of Fortune, Justice, the Hanged Man,
Death, Temperance, the Devil, the Tower, the Star, the Moon, the
Sun, Judgement, the World.

In ROI the deck is painted in a regional medieval-Slavic style —
egg-tempera on wood panel, gold-leaf haloes and frames, Cyrillic
captions in a half-uncial script along the lower edge. Figures wear
the kingdom's own clothing (woollen robes, embroidered tunics,
fieldstone backgrounds) rather than the French/Italian costumes of
the classical Tarot de Marseille.

The deck is shuffled face-down, fanned out, and the Governor draws
one. The drawn card's effect lasts six in-game hours; another draw
isn't permitted for twenty-four hours, regardless of what was drawn.

The cards are *not* a casino tilted in the player's favour:
**41% of draws are pure-positive, 18% are pure-negative, 41% are
trade-offs.** The Fortune Teller is honest and so is the deck.

## 11. Visual Style Guide (for AI Prompts)

When prompting AI image tools for ROI artwork:

**Style anchors that work well:**
- "medieval fantasy concept art, painterly storybook style"
- "Witcher 3 environment art crossed with Studio Ghibli backdrop"
- "warm, slightly weathered, hand-painted"
- "egg-tempera on wood panel" (for tarot / icon / emblem work)
- "Slavic medieval, not Western European medieval" (avoids French
  châteaux and English tudor by default)

**Palette anchors:**
- Forest greens (warm mossy, not cool emerald)
- Weathered timber browns
- Fieldstone greys
- Late-amber gold (the world's signature warm light)
- Oxblood and rust accents (banners, sashes, painted detail)
- Dusk-purple and twilight-blue for magical / fortune teller scenes
- Pale sickly green-yellow ONLY for Blight cues — never elsewhere

**Light anchors:**
- "Golden-hour light from the left" — the default world lighting
- "Soft long shadows, late summer light"
- For dark scenes: "candlelight only, single warm source, deep shadows"
- Never harsh noon sunlight; never flat overcast — the world always
  has direction in its light

**Mood anchors:**
- "Sombre but hopeful — the blight is real but craftsmanship endures"
- "Lived-in, not pristine — wood is worn, stone is moss-streaked"
- "No people in environment shots unless requested" — characters
  break the contemplative tone of location art

**Negative prompt staples (for SD / Flux):**
`people, characters, modern objects, text, watermark, signature,
UI, HUD, oversaturated, anime, cartoon, low detail, French
château, Tudor architecture, Disney princess, generic fantasy
city, neon, plastic`

## 13. Tone — The One-Line Summary

> A patient kingdom of pine, fieldstone and warm amber light, sworn
> to hold back a fevered forest. Craft endures, coin is silver,
> hope is quiet. The Governor was sent by the King because the King
> believed someone would.

Use this as a mood anchor in any prompt where the AI is over-rotating
into grimdark, generic high-fantasy, or Western-European-medieval
clichés.
